1. Executive Summary

This V2 design study compares four still head configurations for a 10-gallon copper pot still, evaluated against compound-specific congener transport, DMS removal, yield, and cost. The key V2 advance is a compound-specific passive reflux model that correctly accounts for heat-loss-driven reflux in packed columns — a phenomenon that disproportionately strips heavy congeners (body, depth compounds) from the distillate.

1.2 Key Finding: Insulation Is Essential

Critical insight: An uninsulated 4" copper column loses ~114W through natural convection and radiation. This creates passive reflux (R≈0.038) that selectively strips heavy congeners — the very compounds (2-phenylethanol, ethyl octanoate, furfural) that give whiskey its body and depth. 25mm fiberglass insulation reduces heat loss to ~16W (R≈0.005), making the CRO column competitive with or superior to a pot still on every desirable compound while maintaining its DMS removal advantage.

2. Physics Models

2.1 Rayleigh Batch Distillation

Recovery of compound i in the hearts cut [f1, f2] from the Rayleigh equation:

Recovery_i = (1 − f1)^E_i − (1 − f2)^E_i where E_i = αrelN_eff

Relative volatility α = Ki/Kethanol governs which compounds concentrate in the distillate (α>1) versus the pot (α<1).[1,2]

2.2 Compound-Specific Passive Reflux

The V2 model introduces compound-dependent effective stages. In a packed column with no external reflux, heat loss from the column wall causes some vapor to condense on the packing surfaces. This condensate preferentially contains heavy compounds (low α) because they have higher dew points. The effective reflux ratio seen by compound i is:

R_eff(i) = R_passive / α_i

N_eff(i) = 1 + N_min × R_eff(i) / (1 + R_eff(i))

Where R_passive = Q_loss / (ΔH_vap × ṁ_vapor) is the overall passive reflux ratio from heat loss, and N_min is the theoretical plates at total reflux from packing HETP.

Physical interpretation: A compound with α=0.10 (2-phenylethanol) sees an effective reflux ratio of R_passive/0.10 = 10× the average. It experiences significantly more theoretical stages and is therefore stripped much more severely than a light compound with α=1.40 (ethyl acetate, R_eff = R/1.4).

2.3 Heat Transfer Model

Column heat loss by combined convection and radiation from an uninsulated (or insulated) vertical cylinder:

  • Convection: h_conv ≈ 7 W/(m²·K) for natural convection on a vertical cylinder with mild air movement (Churchill-Chu gives ~5.5 for still air; 7 accounts for outdoor breeze)[7]
  • Radiation: h_rad = εσ(Tcol² + Tamb²)(Tcol + Tamb) with ε≈0.7 for oxidized Cu
  • Insulation: Cylindrical log-mean resistance R_ins = ln(r2/r1) / (2πkL), where k≈0.04 W/(m·K) for fiberglass. h_rad evaluated at estimated insulation surface temperature.

2.4 PFR Model for CuO-Mediated DMS Removal

DMS removal by CuO on Raschig ring surfaces follows a plug-flow reactor (PFR) model:

Removal = 1 − exp(−k_eff × a_s × L / v_s)

  • k_eff = 0.16 cm/s — lumped mass transfer coefficient (calibrated from pot still data; range 0.08–0.24)
  • a_s = 7.0 cm²/cm³ — specific surface area for 3/8" Cu Raschig rings (≈700 m²/m³)[3]
  • L = 30.5 cm (12" packed bed)
  • v_s ≈ 20 cm/s at 3 kW through 77 cm² cross-section
Important: CuO + DMS → Cu + DMSO is stoichiometric, not catalytic. The oxide layer is consumed during each run and must be regenerated by air oxidation between runs. "CuO-mediated" is the correct descriptor.[4]

4. Recommendation

4.1 For Flavor-Forward Whiskey: CRO Insulated

The CRO insulated variant is the recommended design. It achieves:

  • Best-in-class DMS removal (~80% via PFR, well below 25 ppb threshold)
  • Highest light ester recovery (wider 55% hearts cut vs 40% pot)
  • Heavy congener preservation equal to or better than pot still (insulation eliminates passive reflux penalty)
  • Lower fusel oil carryover than pot still

The additional cost of insulation ($20–30 for fiberglass wrap) pays for itself immediately in flavor quality.

4.2 For Simplicity: Pot + Cu Mesh

If cost and simplicity are primary concerns, the traditional pot still with Cu mesh is a proven design that delivers:

  • Adequate DMS removal (45% from Cu mesh surface area)
  • Excellent heavy congener preservation (no column = no passive reflux)
  • Lowest cost ($80–160)
  • Simplest operation (no column management)

Trade-off: narrower hearts cut means ~30% less yield and less control over light ester selection.

4.3 NOT Recommended: CRO Bare (Uninsulated)

Do not build the CRO column without insulation. The uninsulated configuration loses heavy congeners (2-phenylethanol at 4.9% recovery vs 8.0% insulated) while offering no advantage over the insulated version. The ~$25 insulation cost is the single highest-value modification in this entire design.

6. Safety

Distillation involves flammable vapors, hot surfaces, and potential pressure buildup.
  • PRV on pot lid: Must have pressure relief. Never seal a pot completely.
  • Ventilation: Outdoor operation only, or well-ventilated space. Ethanol vapor is heavier than air and accumulates at floor level.
  • Methanol: Discard the first 100–150 mL (foreshots). Not a significant risk at hobby scale from grain wash, but practice good habits.
  • Carbon monoxide: If using propane heat, ensure adequate ventilation. CO is colorless and odorless.
  • Burns: Column operates at 78–100°C. Insulation reduces burn risk (added benefit).
  • GFCI protection: If using electric heating element, require GFCI on the circuit.
  • Fire extinguisher: Keep a 2A:10B:C rated extinguisher within arm's reach.
  • No open flame near vapor path. Ethanol flash point is 16.6°C.
  • Emergency: If vapor escapes, kill heat immediately. Do not attempt to reseal under pressure.

7. Model Limitations and Uncertainties

  • Relative volatility (α): Values used are approximate, averaged over the hearts cut ABV range. In reality, α varies with ethanol concentration and temperature throughout the run. Cited values are from Léauté (1990) and Piggott (2003), which are for similar conditions but not identical equipment.
  • PFR k_eff: The lumped mass transfer coefficient (0.16 cm/s) is calibrated from pot still DMS removal data, not measured on this specific packing. True values may range from 0.08 to 0.24 cm/s. The uncertainty band in Fig V2-4B reflects this.
  • Passive reflux model: The compound-specific R_eff(i) = R_passive/α_i assumes selective condensation proportional to vapor pressure. This is physically reasonable but simplified — actual condensation depends on binary interaction parameters, surface wetting, and local temperature gradients. The model should predict the direction and approximate magnitude of heavy congener stripping, but absolute numbers have ±30% uncertainty.
  • Heat loss: Natural convection coefficient h=7 W/(m²·K) is calibrated from Churchill-Chu correlation (h≈5.5 for still air on a 0.5m vertical cylinder) with an uplift for outdoor breeze. Actual value depends heavily on ambient air movement; even a 1 m/s breeze can double the coefficient.[7]
  • CuO depletion: The k_eff value assumes a fully oxidized Cu surface. CuO is consumed stoichiometrically during each run (CuO + DMS → Cu + DMSO), so removal efficiency drops as the run progresses. The PFR model uses a run-average. Regeneration between runs (10 min air exposure at 60°C) restores the oxide layer.
  • Fiberglass insulation: Fiberglass wrap near hot Cu surfaces should be silicone-coated or enclosed in foil tape to prevent moisture wicking and fiber degradation. A high-temperature silicone sleeve (rated >200°C) is the premium alternative.
  • Thermal expansion: Cu expands ~17 µm/m/°C. A 0.5m column cycling 22→80°C grows ~0.5mm per heat cycle. Silver-soldered joints can handle this, but threaded fittings need attention. Allow for differential expansion between column and insulation.
  • R_eff model weakness: The R_eff(i) = R_passive/α_i formulation is a first-order approximation. A rigorous treatment would use the Kremser equation with compound-specific equilibrium data. The current model captures the correct qualitative behavior (heavy compounds stripped more) but absolute N_eff values may differ from reality by ±20%.
  • HETP: The Kister wall-effect penalty for d_col/d_pack ≈ 10 is 1.3–1.5× base HETP. Base HETP for 3/8" Raschig is ~250mm (Perry's); with wall penalty, effective HETP is 325–500mm. A 12" (305mm) bed therefore provides ~0.6–1.0 theoretical plates. The model uses N_min=1.0.
