CRO-10 Still Head

Congener Recovery Optimized Design for a 10-Gallon Custom Pot Still

Design goal: Maximize recovery of desirable flavor congeners (esters, furfural, controlled fusels) while chemically eliminating sulfur compounds (DMS, H2S) through copper-packed reactive separation. The CRO-10 recovers 1.3–1.5× more heavy esters than a standard pot still while removing 98.6% of DMS—the best of both worlds.

1. Design Philosophy

1.1 The Pot-vs-Column Tradeoff

Every still designer faces the same fundamental tension:

  • Pot stills produce flavorful but “dirty” spirit. Minimal separation means esters, aldehydes, and higher alcohols all carry into the distillate—along with sulfur compounds like dimethyl sulfide (DMS). A copper pot provides some DMS removal (~80%) but cannot match a packed column.
  • Column stills produce clean but characterless spirit. With 10–20 theoretical plates and high reflux, columns strip virtually all congeners. Vodka-grade purity comes at the cost of losing the very compounds that make whiskey interesting.

1.2 The CRO Solution: Decouple Separation from Sulfur Removal

The CRO-10 design resolves this tradeoff by recognizing that sulfur removal and congener separation are different physical processes:

  • Sulfur removal is chemical: DMS and H2S react with copper surfaces (Cu + DMS → CuS). This requires copper surface area, not theoretical plates. A packed copper column provides 8,800 cm² of reactive surface—17× more than a copper pot alone.
  • Congener separation is physical: Ester recovery depends on reflux ratio, plate count, and cut decisions. A variable dephlegmator (partial reflux condenser) allows real-time tuning: high reflux during foreshots to strip volatiles, zero reflux during hearts to maximize ester passage.

By packing the column with copper Raschig rings instead of stainless steel, we get both functions in a single unit: chemical DMS removal at all reflux settings, and physical separation only when the dephlegmator is actively cooled.

2. Physics & Simulation Results

2.1 Congener Recovery Comparison

The Rayleigh batch distillation model predicts the fraction of each congener that appears in the hearts cut. For component i with enrichment exponent Ei = (αrel,i)Neff, the hearts recovery from fraction f1 to f2 is:

Recoveryi = (1 − f1)Ei − (1 − f2)Ei

The CRO-10 advantage comes from extending the hearts window (12–65% vs the pot still’s typical 15–55%) by using the dephlegmator as a “fusel gate” during late hearts, allowing heavier esters through while retarding fusel alcohols.

2.2 The Five-Phase Reflux Protocol

2.3 Copper Surface Area Optimization

DMS removal follows Langmuir kinetics at the copper surface: removal = A / (A + K½), where K½ ≈ 125 cm². The CRO-10’s 8,800 cm² of copper Raschig rings achieves 98.6% DMS removal—well above the sensory threshold for detection in new-make spirit.

4. Build Instructions

4.1 Tools Required

  • Propane torch (MAPP gas preferred for 3″ joints)
  • Tube cutter for 2″ and 3″ copper pipe
  • Sandpaper/emery cloth (120 grit) for joint prep
  • Step drill or hole saw (3″) for pot lid modification
  • Drill + 1/4″ bit for water port holes
  • Tri-clamp flaring/welding tools (or pre-made TC ferrules)
  • Ruler, protractor, marker

4.2 Step 1: Pot Modification

  1. Mark center of pot lid. Drill pilot hole, then cut 3″ hole with step drill or hole saw.
  2. Insert 3″ TC ferrule from underside. Weld (TIG preferred for SS) or secure with high-temp food-grade silicone sealant + stainless bolts.
  3. Test seal: fill inverted lid with water, check for leaks at ferrule joint.

4.3 Step 2: Column Assembly

  1. Cut pipe: 3″ Cu pipe → two sections: 12″ (vapor rise) and 18″ (packed).
  2. Solder TC ferrules to both ends of each section. Clean pipe ends with emery cloth, apply flux, heat evenly, feed lead-free silver solder. Let cool naturally—do not quench.
  3. Insert mesh screen at bottom of packed section (held by the TC ferrule lip). Pour in 3/8″ Cu Raschig rings to fill. Place second mesh screen on top. Shake gently to settle rings.
  4. Verify: Assemble vapor rise + packed section with TC clamps and gaskets. Check alignment. Blow through to verify airflow is free.

4.4 Step 3: Dephlegmator Construction

  1. Inner tube: Cut 2″ Cu pipe to 8″. Solder the 3″ → 2″ reducer to the bottom end.
  2. Water jacket: Cut 3″ Cu pipe to 8″ (jacket body). Drill two 1/4″ holes on opposite sides: one 1″ from bottom (water inlet), one 1″ from top (water outlet). Solder 1/4″ Cu tube stubs into each hole.
  3. End caps: Cut two 3″ Cu discs (from end cap fittings or sheet). Drill 2″ center holes. Solder inner tube through both end caps. Then solder end caps to jacket body. Critical: ensure all joints are watertight.
  4. Needle valve: Connect 1/4″ brass needle valve to the water inlet stub. Connect water supply line to valve inlet.
  5. Thermocouple: Drill 1/8″ hole at top of inner tube, above water jacket. Insert K-type thermocouple probe. Seal with high-temp silicone or compression fitting.
  6. Pressure test jacket: Cap water outlet, connect water supply to inlet, fill jacket, pressurize to 20 PSI with hand pump. Hold 5 minutes. No leaks = pass.
Fabrication tip: The dephlegmator is the most complex sub-assembly. If you are commissioning a copper fabricator, specify this as a separate deliverable with a hydrostatic pressure test certificate.

4.5 Step 4: Lyne Arm

  1. Cut 2″ Cu pipe to 18″.
  2. Solder 2″ TC ferrule to one end (connects to dephlegmator top).
  3. The other end connects to the condenser. Either solder directly or use another TC fitting for disassembly.
  4. Set angle: When mounted, the lyne arm should slope 10° downward from the dephlegmator toward the condenser. Mark this angle on your mounting structure.

4.6 Step 5: Liebig Condenser

  1. Inner tube: Cut 2″ Cu pipe to 24″.
  2. Jacket: Cut 3″ Cu pipe to 22″. Drill water ports as with dephlegmator: 1/4″ holes for inlet (bottom end) and outlet (top end). Solder 1/4″ tube stubs.
  3. End caps: Same procedure as dephlegmator: 3″ discs with 2″ center holes. Solder inner tube through caps, then solder caps to jacket.
  4. Outlet reducer: Solder 2″ → 1/2″ reducer to the exit end of the inner tube.
  5. Pressure test the water jacket as before.

4.7 Step 6: Final Assembly

  1. Clamp column base (vapor rise) to pot lid TC ferrule with food-grade silicone gasket.
  2. Stack packed section on top of vapor rise via TC clamp.
  3. Mount dephlegmator atop packed section. The 3″ → 2″ reducer at the dephlegmator base mates with the 3″ TC at top of packed section.
  4. Attach lyne arm to dephlegmator top at 10° downslope. Use a support bracket or clamp to a shelf/wall to maintain angle and take weight off the column.
  5. Attach condenser to lyne arm end. Support independently.
  6. Connect water supply: garden hose or bucket pump → needle valve → dephlegmator inlet → dephlegmator outlet → condenser inlet (bottom) → condenser outlet (top) → drain.
  7. Place collection vessel (glass or SS) under condenser outlet with parrot + hydrometer.

4.8 Step 7: Conditioning Run

First run: Charge the pot with a sacrificial wash (cheap sugar wash or cleaning solution of 50:50 water:white vinegar). Run a full distillation to:
  • Flush solder flux residues from all copper joints
  • Season the copper surfaces (initial CuO formation improves sulfur reactivity)
  • Verify all joints are sealed under operating temperature and pressure
  • Practice cut decisions and dephlegmator control
Discard all distillate from the conditioning run.

5. Operating Guide

5.1 Charge and Startup

  1. Charge pot with 8–9 gallons of low wines (~25% ABV) or wash (~10% ABV).
  2. Assemble column, ensure all TC clamps are tight.
  3. Start condenser water flow first (cold water through condenser jacket only).
  4. Apply heat. Bring pot to a slow boil over 30–45 minutes.
  5. When vapor reaches the thermocouple at the dephlegmator top (temperature rises above ambient), you are ready to begin the protocol.

5.2 Five-Phase Reflux Protocol

PhaseThermocoupleDephlegmator ActionDistillate Handling
1. ForeshotsRising rapidly toward 78°CMaximum cooling: open needle valve fully, cold water flows through dephlegmator jacket.Collect first 50–100 mL in small vessel. Smell: nail polish remover / solvent = foreshots. DISCARD.
2. HeadsStabilizing at 78–80°CReduce cooling: close needle valve to ~50%. Moderate reflux.Collect next 500–1000 mL. Fruity + solvent notes. SET ASIDE for blending or recycling.
3. HeartsStable at 80–86°CClose needle valve completely (or switch to warm water). Zero active reflux.COLLECT as new-make spirit. Clean, fruity, cereal character. This is the whiskey.
4. Late hearts86–90°C, risingCrack needle valve open to ~20%. Light reflux slows fusel ingress.Collect or keep separate. Richer, heavier body. Blend into hearts to taste preference.
5. TailsAbove 90°COpen valve to ~60%. Moderate-high reflux.Collect feints for recycling into next run. Fusel-heavy, oily. NEVER add to hearts.
The single most important adjustment: Close the dephlegmator needle valve during hearts. This is the moment that distinguishes the CRO from a standard reflux column. With R ≈ 0, the packed copper section acts purely as a chemical DMS scrubber—zero separation, maximum flavor passage.

5.3 Cut Decision Guides

Three independent signals help you decide when to transition between phases:

  1. Temperature: The thermocouple at the dephlegmator top tracks vapor composition. Rising temperature = heavier compounds arriving. Use the phase table above.
  2. Taste/smell: The traditional test. Dip your finger (clean) or use a smell strip. Foreshots smell like nail polish. Hearts smell clean and grainy. Tails smell waxy/oily.
  3. Hydrometer (in parrot): Hearts typically exit at 68–75% ABV. When ABV drops below 60%, you are entering late hearts/tails.

5.4 Cleaning and Maintenance

  • After every run: Rinse column sections with hot water. Flush water through dephlegmator and condenser jackets. Allow copper to air dry (do not seal wet).
  • Every 5 runs: Remove Raschig rings from packed section. Soak in citric acid solution (2 tbsp per gallon of water) for 30 minutes. Rinse thoroughly. Air dry. Repack.
  • Inspect TC gaskets every 10 runs. Replace if cracked or compressed flat.
  • Copper patina: Green/brown patina on copper surfaces is normal and beneficial (CuO/Cu2O are more reactive with sulfur than bare Cu). Do not polish interior surfaces.

6.3 Integration with Accelerated Maturation

The CRO-10 is designed to produce a new-make spirit optimized for the accelerated aging techniques described in barriers.html. The elevated heavy ester content and reduced DMS mean:

  • Less sulfur to manage during aging (eliminating a major off-flavor source)
  • More ester substrate available for acid-catalyzed transesterification (§1.1, §1.9)
  • Reduced fusel alcohol load means less “hot” character to mask during early maturation
  • The spirit can proceed directly to oxidative maturation without a copper fining step
