## 1. Electrical Conductivity of Wood Biochar

### Key Finding: Wood biochar becomes a reasonable electrical conductor above 600C pyrolysis temperature.

**Gabhi, R.S., Basile, L., Kirk, D.W., Jia, C.Q.** (2020). Electrical conductivity of wood biochar monoliths and its dependence on pyrolysis temperature. *Biochar*, 2, 369-384.
- [Springer Link](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s42773-020-00056-0)

Sugar maple and white pine carbonized via slow pyrolysis at 600, 800, and 1000C. Conductivity measured by 4-probe method:

| Species | 600C | 800C | 1000C |
|---------|------|------|-------|
| Sugar maple | ~1 S/m | ~100 S/m | ~1000 S/m |
| White pine | ~1 S/m | ~50 S/m | ~350 S/m |

The solid carbon consists primarily of disordered carbon with small graphitic nanocrystallites that grow with increasing temperature. Conductivity is correlated with sp2 hybridization of carbon atoms.

**Gabhi, R.S., Kirk, D.W., Jia, C.Q.** (2017). Preliminary investigation of electrical conductivity of monolithic biochar. *Carbon*, 116, 435-442.
- [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0008622317300799)

Bulk conductivity can increase by over six orders of magnitude when carbon content changes from 86.8 to 93.7 wt%. At 600C, conductivity is below 2 S/m. The intrinsic conductivity becomes independent of wood species at 1500C, reaching 14,600 S/m.

**Abdallah, L., Gondran, C., Monnier, V., Vollaire, C., Haddour, N.** (2025). Influence of pyrolysis temperature on the properties and electrochemical performance of cedar wood-derived biochar for supercapacitor electrodes. *Bioengineering (Basel)*, 12(8):841.
- [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12383750/)

Electrical resistivity of cedar biochar:

| Pyrolysis Temp | Resistivity (ohm-m) | Conductivity (S/m) |
|----------------|---------------------|---------------------|
| 800C | 18 x 10^-3 | ~56 |
| 900C | 5.3 x 10^-3 | ~189 |
| 1000C | 3.70 x 10^-3 | ~270 |
| 1100C | 3.60 x 10^-3 | ~278 |

### Relevance to Charred Oak Barrel Staves

Standard bourbon barrel charring reaches approximately 300-500C at the char surface for Level 3-4 char (the "alligator char"), with the actual temperature profile varying through the depth. This is at the LOW END of the conductivity curve -- the char layer is likely a poor conductor at ~1 S/m or below. However, the deeper toasted layer (200-350C) would be essentially non-conductive. A more heavily pyrolyzed char, or supplemental post-charring treatment at 800C+, would be needed to achieve useful conductivity (>50 S/m).

## 2. Surface Area and Porosity of Oak/Hardwood Biochar

**Manya, J.J., Gonzalez, B., Azuara, M., Arner, G.** (2014). Production of biochars by intermediate pyrolysis and activated carbons from oak by three activation methods using CO2. *Journal of Analytical and Applied Pyrolysis*, 109, 139-148.
- [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0165237014000357)

Oak biochar surface areas:

| Pyrolysis Temp | BET Surface Area |
|----------------|-----------------|
| 500C | 107 m2/g |
| 800C | 249 m2/g |
| 800C + CO2 activation | Up to ~1000 m2/g |

**Abdallah et al. (2025)** — Cedar biochar (see above):

| Temp | N2-BET (m2/g) | CO2-BET (m2/g) |
|------|---------------|----------------|
| 800C | 370 | 454 +/- 8 |
| 900C | 1.7 | 385 +/- 30 |
| 1000C | 6.7 | 431 +/- 40 |
| 1100C | 7.3 | 425 +/- 20 |

Note: The dramatic N2-BET collapse above 800C indicates micropore closure at higher temperatures. CO2-BET remains high because CO2 molecules can access ultra-micropores that N2 cannot. This is a critical trade-off: higher pyrolysis temperature gives better conductivity but destroys the macropore/mesopore structure needed for adsorption.

**General findings from the broader literature:**
- Wood biochar has higher specific surface area (124.0 m2/g) than dairy industry biochar (83.4 m2/g)
- Moderate temperatures (400-700C) are optimal for pore structure development
- Oak activated carbon with CO2 activation can reach 1000+ m2/g
- Standard charred barrel char (Level 3-4): estimated 100-300 m2/g surface area based on comparable temperatures

### Charred Barrel Char as Activated Carbon

The whiskey industry already recognizes the char layer's activated carbon function. Sugar maple charcoal used in Tennessee whiskey (Lincoln County process) has a reported surface area of ~300 m2/g. The charred inner surface of a bourbon barrel at approximately 1/3 inch thickness functions as an activated carbon bed during maturation, adsorbing congeners and filtering the spirit.

**Sherwood, K. et al.** (2025). Assessing the extent of charring and its impact on the whisky ageing process. *[Journal TBD]*.
- [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S295006992500009X)

This recent (2025) study directly investigates the relationship between charring extent and whisky aging, confirming that higher charring degree increases surface area exposed to the spirit and promotes formation of flavour congeners via phenol oxidation.

## 3. Biochar Cathodes for H2O2 Electrogeneration (Electro-Fenton)

### Key Finding: Wood-derived biochar can generate H2O2 via 2-electron ORR, directly relevant to the hypothesis.

**Deng, F., Olvera-Vargas, H., Garcia-Rodriguez, O., Zhu, Y., Jiang, J., Qiu, S., Yang, J.** (2019). Waste-wood-derived biochar cathode and its application in electro-Fenton for sulfathiazole treatment at alkaline pH with pyrophosphate electrolyte. *Journal of Hazardous Materials*, 377, 249-258.
- [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31170573/)

Quantitative data:
- Feedstock: Waste wood
- Optimal pyrolysis temperature: 900C
- H2O2 accumulation: **13.80 mg/L in 3 hours**
- Electroactive surface area: 28.81 cm2
- Effective at alkaline pH 8 (with pyrophosphate electrolyte)
- Application: Sulfathiazole degradation

**Zhou, W. et al.** (2021). O-doped graphitic granular biochar enables pollutants removal via simultaneous H2O2 generation and activation in neutral Fe-free electro-Fenton process. *Separation and Purification Technology*, 262:118327.
- [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8340609/)

Quantitative data:
- Feedstock: Bamboo stem (4-8 mesh), pyrolyzed at 1000C, 3h, N2
- BET surface area: 80.9 m2/g (micropore area: 79.3 m2/g)
- Pore volume: 0.0432 cm3/g, average pore diameter: 1.99 nm
- ID/IG ratio (Raman): 0.82 (graphitic)
- H2O2 production from graphitic biochar (GB): **11.3 mg/L from 2.0 g at 50 mA after 50 min, pH 7**
- O-doped biochar (OGB): **18.3 mg/L** (61.2% increase over GB)
- Pollutant removal: RB19 72.6%, Orange II 90.4%, 4-NP 88.2% (120 min)
- TOC mineralization: 48-64% (720 min)
- Long-term stability: 13.2% activity decline after 1500 min (30 cycles)

**Key mechanism:** Graphitic carbon provides conductivity; oxygen-containing surface groups (C=O, C-O-C, O=C-OH) serve as active sites for the 2-electron ORR producing H2O2. No external iron catalyst needed in Fe-free system.

**Nature Water (2025)**: Pulse-driven electrocatalysis with engineered wooden electrode.
- [Nature Water](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44221-025-00466-z)

A self-supporting wooden electrode was fabricated via controlled wood delignification and carbonization, creating a functionalized architecture for efficient oxygen capture from air and two-electron reduction to H2O2. This is the most directly relevant paper -- using actual wood structure as an electrode for H2O2 production.

## 4. Two-Electron ORR on Carbon Materials: Selectivity and Efficiency

**An, J., Feng, Y., Zhao, Q., Wang, X., Liu, J., Li, N.** (2022). Electrosynthesis of H2O2 through two-electron oxygen reduction by carbon-based catalysts. *Environmental Science and Ecotechnology*, 11, 100170.
- [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9488048/)

Comprehensive review of carbon catalyst performance for 2e-ORR:

| Catalyst | H2O2 Selectivity | Faradaic Efficiency |
|----------|-------------------|---------------------|
| N-doped CMK-3 OMC | 98-98.5% | High |
| F-doped HPC | 97.5% | High |
| N,O co-doped OMC | ~95% | High |
| MnO2-carbon black | 96% | High |
| Unmodified carbon black (Vulcan XC-72) | 47-56% | Moderate |
| Oxidized carbon black (air calcined) | 52-56% | Moderate |
| Metal-free plasma-treated carbon black | ~100% | **100%** |

**Critical active site identification:**
- C=O (carbonyl) groups are primary ORR active sites for H2O2
- O=C-OH (carboxyl) has higher intrinsic activity than C=O in alkaline media
- Epoxy/ring ether groups at edge sites achieve "nearly 100% selectivity"
- sp2 carbon sites adjacent to oxide regions dominate ORR activity
- Pyridinic-N and graphitic-N favor the 4-electron pathway (water, not H2O2)

**Implication for charred oak:** The char surface of oak barrels is rich in C=O and C-O-C groups from incomplete pyrolysis of lignin and cellulose. These are precisely the functional groups identified as active sites for 2-electron ORR to H2O2. However, unmodified carbon typically shows moderate selectivity (47-56%); the 4-electron pathway (producing H2O) competes.

## 5. Electrochemical Oxidation of Spirits

### Key Finding: Direct electrochemical oxidation of spirits has been demonstrated and produces aging-like chemical changes.

**Xiong, A., Zhao, K., Hu, Y. et al.** (2020). Influence of electrochemical oxidation on the maturation process of the distilled spirit. *ACS Omega*, 5(29), 18349-18355.
- [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7391936/)

Quantitative results on Baijiu (Chinese distilled spirit):
- Electrodes: Gold working, Pt counter, Ag/AgCl reference
- Applied potentials: 0.2, 0.4, 0.6, 0.8 V
- Treatment duration: 5-20 minutes
- Cyclic voltammetry: Two broad oxidation peaks at [0.45-0.58 V] and [0.63-0.72 V]

Alcohol consumption rates at 0.8 V, 20 min:
- n-Propanol: 4.1 x 10^-4 mg/mL/min
- n-Butyl alcohol: 5.1 x 10^-4 mg/mL/min
- Isoamyl alcohol: 1.5 x 10^-4 mg/mL/min
- n-Hexanol: 3.9 x 10^-4 mg/mL/min

Natural aging rate: <5 x 10^-8 mg/mL/min

**Acceleration factor: ~10,000x faster than natural aging.**

PCA analysis showed "no significant difference between treated new and aged samples" when compared to 1-year naturally aged liquor after 20 min at 0.8 V.

**Critical note:** This study used noble metal (gold) electrodes, not carbon. Carbon electrodes would behave differently -- they have much higher overpotential for alcohol oxidation. However, it establishes the principle that electrochemical oxidation can replicate aging chemistry.

## 6. Fenton Chemistry in Wine/Spirit Matrices

### Key Finding: The Fenton reaction is already recognized as a key mechanism in natural wine/spirit oxidation.

**Elias, R.J., Waterhouse, A.L.** (2010). Controlling the Fenton reaction in wine. *Journal of Agricultural and Food Chemistry*, 58(3), 1699-1707.
- [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20047324/)

Critical quantitative findings:
- Fe3+ (50 uM) + H2O2 (300 uM) in model wine --> **complete stoichiometric conversion to hydroxyl radicals**
- Yields equimolar acetaldehyde (~300 uM) under anaerobic conditions
- Acetaldehyde yield was **depressed in the presence of oxygen** (competing reactions)
- SO2 protects wine by scavenging H2O2 before it enters Fenton pathway

**Ethanol as hydroxyl radical scavenger:**
Rate constant for OH radical + ethanol: **k = 2.2 x 10^9 M^-1 s^-1**

This is extremely fast. In a 40% ethanol spirit, ethanol will scavenge virtually all hydroxyl radicals produced by any Fenton reaction. The primary product is acetaldehyde (and subsequently acetic acid via further oxidation). This means:

1. H2O2 generated at the char cathode surface WILL react with trace Fe/Cu ions in the spirit
2. The resulting hydroxyl radicals WILL be captured by ethanol to form acetaldehyde
3. Acetaldehyde is a KEY intermediate in spirit aging chemistry (forms acetals, reacts with phenolics)
4. The wood-aging system naturally uses tannins to control this oxidation cascade

**The Fenton reaction is already the mechanism by which barrel aging works -- the hypothesis proposes to electrochemically accelerate it.**

## 7. Activated Carbon for Sulfur Compound Adsorption

### Key Finding: Activated carbon effectively adsorbs sulfur compounds from spirits.

**Adsorption capacities for dimethyl sulfide (DMS) on activated carbon:**

| Carbon Type | DMS Capacity | Source |
|-------------|-------------|--------|
| UFZ5 activated carbon | 0.26-0.48 mg/g | Bagreev et al., *Colloids and Surfaces A* |
| KOH-activated sludge carbon | 53.45 mg/g | [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34134443/) |
| Ag-modified AC | Enhanced (highest impact) | Researchgate |
| Coconut shell AC | Standard commercial performance | Industry literature |

For DMDS (dimethyl disulfide): 0.40-0.75 mg/g (UFZ5) to 151.28 mg/g (KOH-activated).

**Spirits industry practice:**
- Activated carbon filtration is already standard in vodka and light rum production
- Wood-based carbon is preferred for whiskey/aged spirits for "subtle filtration without stripping character"
- Coconut shell AC (microporous) is used for aldehyde and sulfide removal
- The Lincoln County process (Tennessee whiskey) uses sugar maple charcoal at ~300 m2/g
- Carbon filtration removes fusel oils, sulfur compounds, fatty acids, and aldehydes

**Key insight:** The charred inner surface of a bourbon barrel already functions as an activated carbon bed. The hypothesis of sulfur adsorption at the char electrode surface is therefore already proven in practice -- the question is whether applying electrical potential enhances or interferes with this adsorption.

## 8. Dual-Function Adsorption + Electrocatalysis

### Key Finding: Simultaneous adsorption and electrochemical degradation on carbon electrodes is well-documented.

**Zhou, W. et al.** (2019). Activated carbon as effective cathode material in iron-free electro-Fenton process: Integrated H2O2 electrogeneration, activation, and pollutants adsorption. *Electrochimica Acta*, 296, 317-326.
- [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30631212/)

Triple-function mechanism on a single activated carbon electrode:
1. **H2O2 electrogeneration** via ORR at oxygen-containing surface groups
2. **H2O2 activation** generating hydroxyl radicals (without external iron)
3. **Organic compound adsorption** via the porous carbon structure

Performance: 8.9 mg/L H2O2 with 1.5 g AC at 100 mA, neutral pH, no external O2 supply.
RB19 degradation: 61.5% (90 min), TOC removal: 74.3% (720 min).

**Synergistic effects in 3D electrode systems:**

When AC particles are placed between electrodes, they become polarized microelectrodes. Adsorbed organic compounds may be oxidized on the positive (anodic) microelectrode surfaces, enabling continuous in-situ regeneration of the activated carbon. This synergy of adsorption + electrocatalysis creates a self-regenerating system.

**This is the closest published analogue to the proposed triple-function hypothesis.** The difference is that published work uses this for wastewater treatment (degrading pollutants), while the hypothesis proposes using it for controlled flavor chemistry (generating desirable intermediates).

## 9. Electroosmotic/Electrokinetic Extraction of Phenolics

### Key Finding: Pulsed electric fields enhance extraction of phenolics from plant biomass.

**Pulsed Electric Field (PEF) literature** (multiple sources, Frontiers in Sustainable Food Systems, 2022):

PEF treatment of plant tissue between electrodes (0.5-10 kV/cm, 1-20 kJ/kg) causes cell membrane electroporation, enhancing extractability of:
- Phenolic compounds: **+15%** vs untreated
- Flavonoids: **+60%** vs untreated
- Tannins: **+42%** vs untreated
- Antioxidant values: **+31%** vs untreated

**Wood-specific transport:**

The macroscopic vertical channel structure in wood (tracheids, vessels) provides effective buffer space for transport and storage of electrolyte ions. Microwave hydrothermal treatment disrupts hydrogen bonds between hemicelluloses and cellulose, and chemical bonds between hemicelluloses and lignin, enabling selective extraction of wood components.

**Electroosmotic flow in porous carbon:**

Porous graphitic carbon supports electroosmotic flow in capillary electrochromatography. The flow rate depends on surface charge (zeta potential) and applied field. In a porous carbon material like charred oak, applying an electric field would drive electroosmotic flow through the pore network, potentially enhancing transport of wood extractives from the intact wood beneath the char layer into the spirit.

**Assessment:** The electroosmotic extraction component of the hypothesis has indirect but meaningful support. PEF-enhanced extraction of phenolics from plant tissue is well-established. Whether low-voltage DC applied through a charred oak stave would create meaningful electroosmotic flow through the char/wood structure is plausible but unproven.

## 10. Wood-Derived Carbon in Supercapacitors (Electrochemical Activity)

### Key Finding: Monolithic wood biochar has demonstrated electrochemical activity.

**Gabhi et al. / Jia group (University of Toronto)**:
Wood biochar monolith supercapacitor work (multiple papers 2020-2024):

- Maple-derived monolithic biochar: **46 F/g at 5 mA/g**, energy density 12.5 Wh/kg, power density 300 W/kg
- Ultra-thick 1 mm biochar monolith (800C): **133 F/g at 10 mA/cm2**, energy density 9.4 Wh/kg
- Basswood-derived electrodes: maximum **223.9 F/g at 2 mA/cm2**
- Intensified wood biochar monolith (compressed pine): bulk density 0.617 g/cm3, pore utilization 28 uF/cm2
- Stability: 93-111% capacitance retention after 10,000 cycles

**Cedar biochar (Abdallah et al., 2025):**
- BC900 specific capacitance: 2.9 F/g at 1 A/g (lower than activated carbon reference at 6.3 F/g)
- BC900 with chitosan binder: >95% capacity retention over 10,000 cycles
- Optimal balance at 900C between conductivity, hydrophobicity, and porosity

These results confirm that wood biochar is electrochemically active -- it can store and transfer charge, which is a prerequisite for any electrode-based process.

## 11. Critical Assessment of the Triple-Function Hypothesis

### The Hypothesis

Charred oak barrel staves, when used as electrodes immersed in spirit, could simultaneously:
- **(a)** Extract wood compounds (vanillin, tannins) via electroosmotic flow
- **(b)** Generate H2O2 at the char surface for controlled Fenton oxidation
- **(c)** Adsorb sulfur compounds (activated carbon function)

### Assessment by Function

#### Function (a): Electroosmotic Extraction of Wood Compounds

**Plausibility: MODERATE**

Supporting evidence:
- PEF extraction of phenolics from plant tissue is established (+15-60% enhancement)
- Wood's tracheid/vessel structure supports ion transport
- Electroosmotic flow in porous carbon is documented

Challenges:
- Barrel char conductivity at standard charring temperatures (~300-500C surface) is very low (~1 S/m)
- The char layer may be too thin and heterogeneous for uniform current distribution
- Ethanol-water mixtures have different dielectric properties than pure water, affecting electroosmotic flow
- The most extractable wood compounds may already be diffusion-limited, not transport-limited

#### Function (b): H2O2 Electrogeneration via 2-Electron ORR

**Plausibility: MODERATE-HIGH (with caveats)**

Supporting evidence:
- Wood-derived biochar cathodes generate H2O2 (13.8-18.3 mg/L demonstrated)
- Carbon surface C=O and C-O-C groups (abundant on charred oak) are the active sites
- Nature Water (2025) demonstrated H2O2 generation on an actual wooden electrode
- The Fenton reaction is already the natural mechanism of spirit oxidation
- Ethanol + hydroxyl radical --> acetaldehyde at k = 2.2 x 10^9 M^-1 s^-1

Critical caveats:
- **Oxygen supply is the rate-limiting factor.** H2O2 generation requires dissolved O2. Spirit in a sealed system has limited dissolved O2 (~8 mg/L saturated in water; lower in ethanol-water). Without continuous O2 supply (e.g., sparging), H2O2 production will be very limited.
- **Ethanol is a hydroxyl radical scavenger.** At 40% ethanol concentration, essentially ALL hydroxyl radicals from the Fenton reaction will react with ethanol. This is actually desirable (produces acetaldehyde) but means there is minimal "free" radical for other oxidation chemistry (e.g., oxidizing tannins directly).
- **Selectivity matters.** Unmodified carbon shows 47-56% selectivity for H2O2 (2e pathway). The rest produces water (4e pathway), wasting current. Modified carbons achieve >95% but require doping.
- **The charred oak is at the wrong pyrolysis temperature for optimal conductivity.** Standard barrel charring (300-500C surface) gives ~1 S/m. The cited studies use 900-1000C biochar at 189-1000 S/m.

#### Function (c): Sulfur Compound Adsorption

**Plausibility: HIGH**

Supporting evidence:
- Activated carbon adsorption of DMS is well-documented (0.26-53 mg/g depending on carbon type)
- The Lincoln County process already uses charcoal for this purpose
- Barrel char already functions as activated carbon during aging
- This is the most established of the three proposed functions

Challenges:
- Applying electrical potential to the carbon may interfere with adsorption. At cathodic (negative) potential, anionic species would be repelled. At anodic potential, neutral species could be desorbed.
- However, polarized AC particles in 3D electrode systems show synergistic adsorption + electrocatalysis, with in-situ regeneration.

### Overall Verdict

**The hypothesis has partial basis in published science, but faces significant practical barriers.**

#### What is well-supported:
1. Charred wood IS biochar, and biochar IS an electrode material
2. Carbon electrode surfaces DO generate H2O2 via 2-electron ORR
3. The Fenton reaction IS the natural mechanism of spirit oxidation
4. Activated carbon (charcoal) DOES adsorb sulfur compounds from spirits
5. Electrochemical oxidation DOES accelerate spirit maturation (~10,000x in one study)
6. Dual-function adsorption + electrocatalysis on carbon is proven in wastewater treatment

#### What is problematic:
1. **Conductivity gap:** Standard barrel char (~300-500C) has conductivity ~1 S/m. Published biochar electrode work uses 800-1000C material at 50-1000 S/m. The char layer would need to be produced at higher temperatures or the system would need very high voltages to drive meaningful current.

2. **Oxygen limitation:** H2O2 generation requires dissolved O2. A closed spirit vessel has very limited O2. Without active O2 supply (bubbling, gas diffusion electrode), H2O2 production is negligible.

3. **Ethanol scavenging:** 40% ethanol will capture all hydroxyl radicals, converting them to acetaldehyde. While this is a desired aging reaction, it means the system cannot selectively oxidize other targets (tannins, phenolics) -- ethanol dominates the radical chemistry.

4. **No direct precedent:** No published study applies biochar electrochemistry in an ethanol-water matrix for flavor chemistry. All electro-Fenton literature uses aqueous systems for wastewater treatment.

5. **Electrode geometry:** A barrel stave is thick (2 cm+), with char only on one face (~8 mm). Current distribution through this heterogeneous structure would be highly non-uniform.

### Proposed Modifications to Improve Feasibility

1. **Use separately produced high-temperature biochar (800-900C oak) as the cathode**, not the barrel char directly. This could be produced as inserts or a porous plug.

2. **Incorporate a gas-diffusion layer** or O2 sparging to supply dissolved oxygen for H2O2 generation.

3. **Use a two-electrode cell** with the biochar cathode (H2O2 generation) and a separate inert anode (direct oxidation), both immersed in spirit with oak chips for extraction.

4. **Accept the ethanol scavenging** as a feature, not a bug: the primary product (acetaldehyde) is the key intermediate in acetal formation (diethyl acetal = delicate fragrance "top note") and phenolic-aldehyde condensation reactions.

5. **Quantify the trade-off** between adsorption capacity and electrochemical activity as a function of applied potential, since these may partially conflict.
